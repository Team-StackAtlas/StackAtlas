# Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 2026-10-20**

StackAtlas is a community platform for information about supplements, peptides, and nootropics. This policy explains what we collect, what we deliberately do not collect, and the choices you have. It is written to be read, not skimmed. Where we use the words "we" or "us," we mean the operator of StackAtlas (⟪OPERATOR LEGAL NAME AND ADDRESS⟫).

## The short version

- We do **not** track what you read, search for, or look at. There are no analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs on this site.
- Your **access level** (the "Citizen" or "Explorer" setting that controls which substance tiers you see) is stored only in your own browser. It is never sent to our servers.
- We do **not** store your IP address or device identifiers in our database, and we never use them to build a profile of you.
- We do **not** sell, rent, or share personal data with advertisers or data brokers. We have no advertisers.
- You can delete your account and data from inside the app.

## 1. What we collect, and why

We collect only what the features you use require. Everything below is tied to your account **only if you create one**. You can browse StackAtlas without an account, in which case nothing in this section applies to you.

### 1.1 Account

- **Email address and password.** Used to sign you in. Passwords are hashed by our authentication provider and are never visible to us.
- **Username and display name.**

### 1.2 Profile (all optional)

You choose whether to add any of these: avatar image URL, bio, website, and self-reported body statistics (age, sex, height, weight, body-fat percentage). Each has its own visibility switch in your profile settings, and all default to hidden. We store them so the features you turned them on for can work (for example, showing your stats on your profile if you chose to).

### 1.3 Goals

At onboarding you can pick goal categories (for example, "Cognition" or "Recovery") so the feed can rank content for you. If you are signed in, your goals are saved to your profile so they follow you across devices. If you are not signed in, they stay on your device only.

### 1.4 Things you create or do

Posts, comments, votes, brand and product ratings, stacks you publish, library albums, items you save or hide, accounts you follow, direct messages, and reports you file. This is the content of the service; storing it is the point of it. Your public posts and ratings are visible to other users under your username.

### 1.5 Preferences

Notification preferences and, if you turn it on, an opt-in for product update emails (off by default; no emails are sent unless you opt in).

### 1.6 Moderation and safety records

When a moderator or administrator acts on content or an account, that action is logged with the acting administrator's identity and the affected item. We keep these records to run a fair, auditable moderation process.

## 2. What we deliberately do not collect

This section exists because many of our users live in places where even reading about certain substances is sensitive. We built the site so that your reading is nobody's business.

- **No reading, viewing, or search history.** We do not log which substance, brand, or post pages you open, what you type into search, or which filters you use. There is no analytics service — first-party or third-party — on this site.
- **No access-level tracking.** The Citizen/Explorer setting and the classification tiers it reveals (Everyday, Clinical, Frontier) are stored in your browser's local storage and are never transmitted to us. Clearing your browser storage removes them.
- **No IP addresses or device fingerprints in our database.** See Section 4 for the one place IP addresses necessarily appear.
- **No advertising identifiers, no third-party cookies, no social-media pixels, no session-replay tools.** We audited our code for third-party software development kits; there are none.
- **No location data.**

## 3. Aggregate statistics

We may compute aggregate numbers from the actions people take on the site — for example, how many users have saved a substance, or how a brand's average rating has moved. These counts are derived from actions, not from browsing, and nothing we publish or use internally from them identifies you. Your individual votes and ratings remain attached to your account so that you can change or remove them, and so that one account cannot vote twice.

## 4. Infrastructure providers (the one honest caveat about IP addresses)

StackAtlas runs on two infrastructure providers:

- **Supabase** (database and authentication), hosted in the United States (us-west-1).
- **Vercel** (web hosting and content delivery).

Like every web service, these providers receive your IP address and basic request metadata in order to deliver pages to you and to protect against abuse (for example, blocking brute-force sign-in attempts). Our authentication provider keeps standard security logs of sign-in events that include IP addresses for a limited time. These logs are operated by the provider for security, are not copied into our application database, and are never used by us to profile or track you. Both providers act as processors on our behalf under their standard data-processing terms.

We do not use any other third party to process your personal data.

## 5. Legal bases (for users in the EU, UK, and similar jurisdictions)

- **Performance of a contract:** account, profile, content, preferences — the things the service cannot work without.
- **Legitimate interests:** moderation records, security logs kept by our providers, and aggregate statistics; we have balanced these against your interests and kept them to the minimum.
- **Consent:** optional profile fields, goal syncing, and product-update emails. You can withdraw consent at any time by removing the data or turning the option off.

## 6. Your rights and controls

You can exercise all of these without contacting us, from inside the app:

- **Access and correction:** edit your profile, posts, and settings at any time.
- **Visibility:** every optional profile field has its own show/hide switch; your account can be set to private.
- **Deletion:** Profile → Account → *Delete my account and data*. This removes your personal profile fields immediately, soft-deletes your content, and queues your account for permanent erasure. See Section 7 for timing.
- **Portability:** email us (Section 10) and we will provide a machine-readable copy of your data.
- **Objection and restriction:** turn off goal syncing by signing out of the device, or contact us.

If you are in the EU/UK you may also lodge a complaint with your supervisory authority. If you are a California resident, the rights above cover your rights to know, delete, and correct; we do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined under California law, so there is nothing to opt out of.

## 7. Retention

- Account and profile data: kept while your account exists.
- Content you delete: hidden immediately and permanently removed within 30 days.
- Accounts you delete: personal fields are erased immediately; the account and its remaining records are permanently removed within 30 days. Posts may be retained in anonymized form (author shown as "deleted user") where removing them would break other users' conversations.
- Moderation records: retained for as long as necessary to run the moderation process, then deleted.
- Provider security logs: retained by the provider for its standard period, typically no more than 90 days.
- Backups: encrypted database backups are kept for up to 30 days and then expire, after which deleted data is gone from backups too.

## 8. Security

Data is encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest by our providers. Database access is governed by row-level security policies so that users can only read what they are permitted to read. Administrative actions are logged. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach affecting your data we will notify you as the law requires.

## 9. Children

StackAtlas is for adults. You must be at least 18 years old (or the age of majority where you live, if higher) to use it. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18; if we learn we have, we delete it.
